# The Crew: Mission Deep Sea

## Game Guide and Rules Reference

> **Game:** *The Crew: Mission Deep Sea*  
> **Designer:** Thomas Sing  
> **Publisher:** KOSMOS / Thames & Kosmos  
> **Players:** 3–5 normally, with a special 2-player variant  
> **Recommended age:** 10+  
> **Type:** Cooperative trick-taking card game, mission-based campaign  
> **Theme:** Deep-sea exploration

---

## 1. Overview

*The Crew: Mission Deep Sea* is a cooperative card game in which the players are members of the same underwater expedition. Rather than competing to collect the most points or win the most tricks, the entire crew works together to satisfy the objectives of the current mission.

The game is built around **trick-taking**, a style of card play found in games such as Hearts, Spades, or Bridge. Each player contributes one card to a trick, one player wins that trick, and the winner leads the next one. The major difference is that in *The Crew*, winning a trick is not automatically good. The correct player must win the correct cards, at the correct time, in order for the team to complete its tasks.

The central challenge is communication. Players normally may **not reveal or describe the cards in their hands**. Each crew member has only a very limited sonar communication system, so the team must infer what others are holding from the cards that have already been played, the tasks each player accepted, and the few legal pieces of information that have been communicated.

Each mission introduces a particular combination of objectives or special rules. Missions become progressively more demanding, but failed missions can simply be attempted again.

*Mission Deep Sea* is a standalone game. You do not need *The Crew: The Quest for Planet Nine* to play it.

---

## 2. Objective of the Game

The crew wins a mission when **all required tasks and mission conditions have been completed**.

The crew loses a mission as soon as an objective becomes impossible to complete, or when the cards run out without all required conditions being satisfied.

There are no individual winners. Either the entire crew succeeds or the entire crew fails.

The broader game is a sequence of missions recorded in the logbook. You may play them as a campaign, replay missions, or select individual missions if your group prefers.

---

## 3. Contents of the Box

The game contains:

- **40 large playing cards**
  - 36 color cards
    - 4 suits/colors
    - values **1 through 9** in each color
  - 4 submarine cards
    - values **1 through 4**
- **5 large reminder cards**
- **96 small task cards**
- **5 sonar tokens**
- **1 distress signal token**
- **1 captain token**
- **1 rulebook**
- **1 logbook**

The four normal colors are represented not only by color but also by distinct symbols, which helps players distinguish suits regardless of color perception.

---

## 4. The Playing Cards

### 4.1 Color Cards

There are four normal suits. Each contains the numbers 1 through 9.

Within one suit, a higher number beats a lower number.

For example, if blue is the suit that was led, a blue 8 beats a blue 3.

### 4.2 Submarine Cards

The four submarine cards, numbered 1 through 4, form a special fifth suit and act as **trump cards**.

A submarine normally beats every color card in a trick, regardless of the color card's value.

If more than one submarine is played in the same trick, the highest submarine wins.

Submarine cards are also a suit of their own. If a trick is opened with a submarine and you have at least one submarine in your hand, you must follow with a submarine.

### 4.3 The Captain

The player holding the **submarine 4** is the captain for that mission attempt.

Give that player the captain token.

The captain has several important responsibilities:

- selecting the first task during normal task distribution;
- leading the first trick of the mission;
- resolving certain mission-specific instructions when the logbook says the captain must do so.

The captain is still a normal crew member and follows all normal rules unless a mission explicitly says otherwise.

---

## 5. What Is a Trick?

A **trick** is one round in which every active crew member plays one card.

The player who starts the trick is called the leader. The suit of the card played by the leader becomes the **led suit** for that trick.

Going clockwise, every other player plays one card.

### Following Suit

If you have at least one card of the led suit, you **must** play a card of that suit.

If you do not have any card of the led suit, you may play any card from your hand, including a submarine.

You are never required to play your highest card or deliberately win a trick.

### Determining the Winner

If no submarine was played, the highest card of the led suit wins the trick.

Cards of other colors cannot win the trick, even if their numerical value is higher.

If one or more submarines were played, the highest submarine wins the trick.

The winner takes all cards from the trick and places the trick face down in front of themselves. That player leads the next trick.

Normally, only the most recently completed trick may be looked at again.

---

## 6. Standard Setup for 3–5 Players

Before each mission attempt:

1. Shuffle all **40 playing cards**.
2. Deal them face down as evenly as possible among all players.
   - With 4 players, everyone receives 10 cards.
   - With 5 players, everyone receives 8 cards.
   - With 3 players, the cards cannot be divided equally. One player receives one extra card; after the final full trick, one card will remain unplayed.
3. Each player takes one sonar token and places it **green side up** in front of themselves.
4. Keep one reminder card available for each player.
5. Place the distress signal token with its inactive side showing.
6. Shuffle the 96 task cards and place them nearby as a face-down deck.
7. Read the current mission in the logbook and follow any mission-specific setup instructions.

Mission rules override the standard rules whenever the mission explicitly says so.

---

## 7. Preparing the Mission Tasks

Most missions use task cards.

Each task card describes something that the player who receives it must accomplish. Unlike the first *Crew* game, *Mission Deep Sea* uses a wide variety of objective types rather than relying mainly on capturing one specific card.

### 7.1 Mission Difficulty

A mission normally specifies a total **difficulty value**.

Each task card has a difficulty value that depends on whether the crew contains 3, 4, or 5 players.

Use only the value corresponding to the current number of crew members.

Draw task cards until the sum of their difficulty values is **exactly equal** to the mission's required difficulty.

If the next card would make the total too high, skip it and continue looking for a task of a suitable value.

Example:

- Mission difficulty: 5
- First task: difficulty 2
- Second task: difficulty 1
- Current total: 3
- A difficulty-3 card would raise the total to 6, so it must be skipped.
- The crew needs another 2 points of task difficulty, such as one difficulty-2 task or two difficulty-1 tasks.

Once the required total has been reached, place the selected task cards face up in the center of the table.

---

## 8. Assigning Tasks

Unless the mission gives different instructions, task cards are drafted by the crew.

1. Identify the captain: the player holding submarine 4.
2. The captain chooses the first available task and places it face up in front of themselves.
3. Continue clockwise. Each player chooses one available task.
4. Continue around the table until every task has been assigned.

A player may receive multiple tasks, and some players may receive none.

### Passing During Task Selection

Passing is permitted only when, at the **beginning of task selection**, there are fewer task cards than crew members.

If passing is allowed, a player may decline to take a task on their turn. However, by the time every crew member has had one opportunity to select or pass, **all tasks must have been assigned**.

If the number of task cards is equal to or greater than the number of players, nobody may pass.

### No Communication Yet

When task cards are used, the crew may not use sonar communication until **after all tasks have been assigned**.

Players also may not secretly explain why they do or do not want a particular task. The limitations on information about cards in hand remain in force.

---

## 9. Communication Rules

Communication is deliberately restricted.

### 9.1 What You May Not Say

You may not tell, show, hint, gesture, or otherwise reveal information that is known only because of the cards in your hand.

Illegal examples include statements such as:

- "I have the yellow 9."
- "I have no blue cards."
- "I can win the next trick."
- "Do not lead green; I have something important there."
- "Give me this task because my hand is perfect for it."

The basic principle is simple:

> If the information comes from knowledge of your hidden hand, you cannot communicate it unless the sonar rules specifically allow you to do so.

Public information may, of course, be discussed when the mission permits normal discussion. This includes information visible on the table or already established by previous play.

### 9.2 Sonar Communication

Normally, each player may communicate **once per mission attempt** by using their sonar token.

Communication may happen before any trick, including between tricks, but never while a trick is in progress.

If task cards are present, all tasks must be assigned first.

To communicate:

1. Choose one **color card** from your hand.
2. Place it face up in front of you.
3. Place your sonar token on that card in one of three positions:
   - **Top:** this is your highest card of that color.
   - **Middle:** this is your only card of that color.
   - **Bottom:** this is your lowest card of that color.
4. Take a reminder card into your hand so your total hand size remains visually unchanged.

The communicated card is still part of your hand and must eventually be played normally.

When you play that card, discard the reminder card.

### 9.3 Restrictions on Sonar Communication

The card must genuinely satisfy the meaning of the token position at the moment you communicate it.

A card that is neither your highest, lowest, nor only card of that color cannot be communicated.

Submarine cards can **never** be used for standard sonar communication.

Once the sonar token has been placed, its position does not change later, even if subsequent card play would make the original message no longer literally true.

---

## 10. Starting and Playing a Mission

After all tasks have been assigned and any pre-mission effects are resolved:

1. The captain leads the first trick by playing any legal card from their hand.
2. Every other player plays one card clockwise, following suit when possible.
3. Determine the winner of the trick.
4. The winner collects the trick.
5. Check whether the trick completes, advances, or fails any task.
6. The winner of the trick leads the next trick.
7. Continue until all objectives have been completed, an objective has become impossible, or no more full tricks can be played.

Good play is not about winning as many tricks as possible. Often, the most important move is deliberately losing a trick so that another crew member receives the cards they need.

---

## 11. Completing Tasks

A task belongs only to the player who selected or received it.

A task is considered complete when its requirements have been satisfied **and can no longer become false later in the mission**.

When a task is permanently complete, turn its task card face down.

A single trick can sometimes satisfy several tasks at once.

Some tasks are naturally checked only at the end of the mission because later tricks could still change the result. Examples include objectives involving an exact number of tricks, comparative totals, or cards that a player must avoid winning.

If even one required task becomes impossible, the mission has failed.

---

## 12. Types of Task Cards

The 96 task cards create many different kinds of objectives. The exact wording on the card is always authoritative.

Common task families include the following.

### 12.1 Win Particular Cards

A player may need to win:

- a specific numbered card;
- any card of a particular color;
- any card of a particular number;
- one or more submarine cards;
- several specified cards across one or more tricks.

If multiple required cards are shown, they do not necessarily have to be won in the same trick unless the task explicitly says so.

### 12.2 Win a Trick Using a Particular Card

The task may require the player to win a trick by playing a particular value, color, or type of card.

This is different from merely collecting that card in a trick.

### 12.3 Avoid Particular Cards

A task may require that the player finish the mission without having won any card of a specified value, color, or type.

These objectives are generally not safely complete until the mission is over.

### 12.4 Win a Certain Number of Tricks

A task may require:

- exactly a certain number of tricks;
- at least a certain number of tricks;
- a number of consecutive tricks;
- particular early or late tricks;
- only certain tricks and no others.

Pay close attention to words such as **exactly**, **at least**, **only**, and **in a row**.

### 12.5 Compare Trick Counts

Some tasks compare how many tricks one crew member wins relative to another.

Certain captain-comparison tasks contain restrictions on whether the captain may select them. Follow the task card and rulebook symbol exactly.

### 12.6 Trick Value Conditions

Some tasks care about the numerical values of the color cards inside a trick.

For example, a trick may need to have:

- a total value above, below, or equal to a target;
- only cards above a certain value;
- only cards below a certain value;
- only odd-numbered cards;
- only even-numbered cards.

Some of these tasks prohibit submarines from appearing in the relevant trick.

### 12.7 Color and Collection Conditions

Other tasks may require a player to:

- collect cards from every color;
- collect all cards of at least one color;
- collect more cards of one color than another;
- satisfy a particular combination of colors or values.

Because *Mission Deep Sea* has many task designs, do not assume that two cards with similar illustrations have identical timing or completion rules. Read the full task text.

---

## 13. Impossible or Unreasonable Task Combinations

Because tasks are generated from a large randomized task deck, some combinations can be impossible.

For example, two different players cannot both be required to win the same unique trick if the tasks demand mutually exclusive results.

When a newly revealed task creates an objectively impossible combination regardless of sensible assignment, replace the problematic task according to the rulebook procedure with another task of the same difficulty.

Some rare impossibilities depend on the actual deal of the playing cards. If a task is impossible solely because of an unavoidable card distribution specifically covered by the rules, redeal rather than counting it as a failed attempt.

However, a bad task assignment chosen by the players is not automatically an impossible setup. Evaluating whether a task is realistically achievable is part of the game.

---

## 14. Failure and Retrying a Mission

If any task can no longer be completed, the mission fails immediately.

When retrying:

1. Shuffle and redeal the playing cards.
2. Reset sonar tokens.
3. Reset mission-specific elements as required.
4. For the task cards, the crew may normally either retry the same set or replace them with newly drawn tasks, unless the mission says otherwise.

Record attempts in the logbook according to the campaign instructions.

A failure is not a permanent loss. Missions are designed to be replayed, and later attempts often become easier as the group learns how to interpret each other's decisions.

---

## 15. Distress Signal — Optional Assistance

The distress signal is an optional help mechanism.

It may be activated at the start of a mission attempt **after playing cards and tasks have been assigned but before anyone uses sonar communication**.

If the crew activates it:

1. Turn the distress signal token to its active side.
2. Record its use for the mission in the logbook as instructed.
3. The crew chooses one direction: left or right.
4. Every player passes exactly one card to the neighboring player in that direction.
5. All players must pass in the same direction.
6. **Submarine cards may not be passed.**

Either everyone passes a card or nobody does.

Once activated, the distress signal remains available for subsequent attempts at that same mission until the mission is completed. At the beginning of each new attempt, the crew may again use the card-passing benefit under the same restrictions.

Using the distress signal affects the recorded attempt count as described in the official logbook rules, but every mission can be completed without it.

---

## 16. Mission-Specific Rules

The logbook sometimes changes the normal rules. These changes may affect areas such as:

- how task cards are distributed;
- which players are allowed to take tasks;
- how many sonar communications the entire group receives;
- whether communication is modified or restricted;
- whether a random color changes a rule;
- whether players may discuss task allocation more freely;
- time limits;
- special requirements about who leads or wins certain tricks.

Always read the mission entry before dealing with its tasks.

If a mission rule contradicts a general rule in this guide, the mission rule takes priority for that mission.

## 17. Two-Player Variant

Two players can play by adding a simulated third crew member called **Tonoja**.

### 17.1 Setup

1. Set the submarine 4 aside temporarily.
2. Shuffle the other 39 playing cards.
3. Place **7 cards face down** in a horizontal row.
4. Place **7 cards face up**, one on top of each face-down card.
   - These 14 cards form Tonoja's hand.
5. Return submarine 4 to the remaining cards and shuffle.
6. Deal the remaining cards equally between the two human players.
   - Each human player receives 13 cards.
7. Each human player takes a sonar token and reminder card.
8. Place the distress signal token inactive.
9. Prepare the task deck normally.

For tasks and mission difficulty, treat the game as a **3-player game** because Tonoja counts as the third crew member.

### 17.2 Captain and Tonoja

The human player who receives submarine 4 is the captain.

In addition to the captain's normal duties, that player controls Tonoja.

The captain:

- chooses tasks for Tonoja during task assignment;
- decides which card Tonoja plays on Tonoja's turn;
- makes those decisions without discussing them with the other human player.

### 17.3 Playing Tonoja's Cards

Tonoja may play only cards that are currently face up.

Normal follow-suit rules still apply.

When one of Tonoja's face-up cards is played, the face-down card underneath it is turned face up **after the trick is finished**, never during the trick.

Tonoja otherwise counts as a normal crew member for trick-taking and task completion.

## 18. Example of a Trick

Assume four players are in a mission.

The first player leads with a **green 3**.

- Player A: green 3
- Player B: green 5
- Player C: yellow 9
- Player D: green 2

Green is the led suit.

Player C was allowed to play yellow only if they had no green card.

Even though yellow 9 has the highest printed number, it cannot win because it is not green. Player B wins with green 5.

Now consider the same trick if Player C instead plays **submarine 1**. The submarine is trump, so Player C wins despite its low numerical value.

---

## 19. Example of Sonar Communication

Suppose your yellow cards are:

- yellow 2
- yellow 6
- yellow 8

You may legally communicate:

- yellow 2 as your **lowest yellow** card;
- yellow 8 as your **highest yellow** card.

You may not communicate yellow 6 because it is neither your highest, lowest, nor only yellow card.

If yellow 8 were your only yellow card, you would place the sonar token in the **middle** rather than at the top.

You cannot communicate any submarine card.

---

## 20. Example of Cooperative Planning

Suppose one player has a task requiring them to win a trick containing a particular card.

The other players cannot simply ask who holds the cards needed to arrange the trick. Instead, the team has to work from legal information:

- who accepted the task;
- which suits players have failed to follow during earlier tricks;
- which high or low cards have already been played;
- any legal sonar communication;
- who currently controls the lead.

A player may intentionally lead a low card, sacrifice a high card, or avoid using a submarine in order to transfer control of the next trick to the correct crew member.

This inference-based cooperation is the core of the game.

## 21. Strategy Principles for New Crews

These are not additional rules, but they help explain how the game is meant to be approached.

### Preserve Useful Leads

Winning a trick also means leading the next one. Sometimes the most important reason to win a trick is not the cards in it, but the ability to choose the next suit.

### Track Missing Suits

If a player fails to follow a suit, everyone now knows publicly that the player had no cards of that suit at that moment. This information can be extremely valuable later.

### Do Not Waste Submarines

Submarines are powerful because they can take control of a trick, but there are only four of them. Using one too early can make a later task impossible.

### Think Before Accepting a Task

The task-selection phase is already part of the puzzle. The cards in your hand may make one task far more suitable for you than another, even though you cannot explain why.

### Use Sonar for Information, Not Confirmation

A good sonar message often tells the crew something that changes how several future tricks can be played. Revealing an obvious card simply to confirm what others already suspect can waste your only normal communication.

### Remember That Losing a Trick Can Be Correct

This is a cooperative game. A carefully planned loss is often more valuable than winning.

## 22. Common Rules Mistakes

### Mistake: "The highest number always wins."

No. Without a submarine, only cards of the led suit can win.

### Mistake: "I can play any card I want."

Only if you have no card of the led suit. Otherwise you must follow suit.

### Mistake: "Submarines can be played at any time."

Only if doing so respects follow-suit. If a color was led and you still have that color, you cannot discard a submarine instead.

### Mistake: "I must try to win the trick."

No. You only have to follow suit. You may choose any legal card of that suit.

### Mistake: "We can discuss our hands as long as we don't name exact cards."

No. Indirectly revealing hidden hand information is also prohibited.

### Mistake: "The sonar token means I can reveal any card."

No. The card must be your highest, lowest, or only card of its color, and it cannot be a submarine.

### Mistake: "Once I communicate a card, it leaves my hand."

No. It remains part of your hand and will later be played normally. The reminder card simply preserves the apparent hand size.

### Mistake: "Everyone must receive a task."

No. Tasks are not necessarily distributed evenly, and a player may have no task at all.

### Mistake: "Winning my own tasks is enough."

No. The mission succeeds only when the entire crew completes every required objective.

## 23. Quick Rules Reference

### Before the Mission

1. Read the mission.
2. Shuffle and deal the 40 playing cards.
3. Give each player a sonar token and prepare reminder cards.
4. Prepare the distress signal token.
5. Draw task cards until their difficulty totals exactly the mission's required value.
6. The holder of submarine 4 becomes captain.
7. Assign all tasks, beginning with the captain.
8. Optionally activate the distress signal before any sonar communication.
9. Players may now use legal sonar communication.

### During Each Trick

1. The leader plays one card.
2. Every other player follows suit if able.
3. If no submarine is played, highest card of the led suit wins.
4. If submarines are played, highest submarine wins.
5. The winner takes the trick.
6. Resolve task progress.
7. The winner leads the next trick.

### Sonar

- Normally once per player per mission attempt.
- Only before a trick, never during it.
- After task assignment.
- Reveal one color card.
- Token at top = highest of that color.
- Token in middle = only card of that color.
- Token at bottom = lowest of that color.
- No submarine cards.

### Mission Result

- **Success:** every task and special mission condition is completed.
- **Failure:** any required objective becomes impossible or remains incomplete when play ends.

## 24. First-Game Teaching Order

For a group that has never played a trick-taking game, teach the game in this order:

1. Explain that everyone is on the same team.
2. Demonstrate one ordinary trick using only color cards.
3. Explain following suit.
4. Add submarines and explain trump cards.
5. Explain that the trick winner leads next.
6. Introduce one simple task.
7. Explain that the player who owns the task must satisfy it.
8. Explain the ban on discussing hidden cards.
9. Demonstrate the three sonar-token positions.
10. Play the introductory mission before explaining every advanced task type.

The mission system is intentionally progressive. It is usually easier to learn the advanced rules as the logbook introduces them rather than teaching every possible task and modifier at once.

---

## 25. Summary

*The Crew: Mission Deep Sea* turns a traditional trick-taking card system into a cooperative logic puzzle.

The essential rules are:

- follow the suit that was led whenever possible;
- submarines are trump cards;
- the winner of a trick leads the next one;
- the holder of submarine 4 is the captain;
- tasks determine which tricks the crew needs to engineer;
- players cannot freely discuss their hidden cards;
- sonar allows each player a very limited, precisely defined message;
- everyone wins or loses together.

The challenge comes from combining these simple rules with increasingly unusual mission objectives. Successful crews learn to read the table, preserve useful cards, infer what others can and cannot hold, and communicate through play rather than conversation.
